# Hello, World! 👋

*The first post. The beginning of something.*

Hey there! I'm **Sai Puneeth**, a tech enthusiast from Hyderabad, India.

Welcome to my little corner of the internet!

I've always been fascinated by technology, not necessarily because of what it can do, but because of what goes on behind the scenes. I'm naturally curious about how things work, why they work the way they do, and what's happening underneath the surface.

I love the idea of taking something that looks incredibly complex from the outside, breaking it down, understanding its pieces, and eventually figuring out how everything comes together.

That's pretty much what got me into technology.

But my journey didn't exactly start with a computer science textbook or a fancy programming setup. It started somewhere quite different.

## It all started with curiosity

Before I got seriously interested in technology, I was fascinated by airplanes.

I used to wonder how something so incredibly heavy could fly so effortlessly through the sky. I wanted to understand the science behind it, so I started researching things on my own. Eventually, I became so interested that I wanted to pursue aviation and become a pilot.

I even brought up the idea with my parents.

Their response? Something along the lines of, *"Why do you want to become a driver?"*

Well, that was an interesting way to look at it!

After doing some research, I also discovered that flight school was quite expensive. So, unfortunately, the pilot dream had to take a back seat.

Fortunately, I had another interest growing alongside it: technology.

And that's where things started getting interesting.

## From Android tinkering to breaking things (and fixing them)

My earliest experiments with technology were mostly around Android smartphones.

I wasn't particularly interested in using my phone the way an ordinary user would. I wanted to explore what was possible beyond the usual settings and restrictions.

I started experimenting with APKs, patching applications using Lucky Patcher, rooting my phone, exploring custom ROMs, and playing around with custom kernels.

I even remember connecting a PS3 controller to my Android phone using an OTG adapter and figuring out how to play games with it.

It might sound like a bunch of random experiments, but those little experiments introduced me to an entirely different side of technology.

I began realizing that there was so much more happening underneath the interface we interact with every day.

And naturally, I wanted to explore more.

## The laptop era and my introduction to Linux

Then came the lockdown.

Online classes meant I finally had to spend more time with my laptop. Back then, I had an old machine powered by a second-generation Intel Core i3 processor, with 6 GB of RAM and a traditional hard disk.

No SSD. No fancy hardware. Just an old laptop trying its best!

It was painfully slow, but instead of accepting its limitations, I decided to experiment with it.

I started by exploring Windows modifications. I moved between different Windows versions, tried lightweight and optimized builds, and even figured out ways to install Windows 11 on hardware that wasn't officially supported.

I spent quite some time tweaking and optimizing Windows until I managed to make the laptop reasonably usable.

But eventually, I stumbled upon something that would completely change how I use computers.

**Linux.**

I started with Linux Mint. Things weren't exactly smooth initially. I ran into problems with brightness controls, Wi-Fi adapters, and a bunch of other hardware-related issues.

But every problem became another opportunity to learn.

I gradually moved between distributions, exploring Ubuntu, Debian, and eventually Arch Linux.

And then I discovered Hyprland.

That keyboard-driven, highly customizable desktop experience absolutely blew my mind. The idea of controlling almost everything through the keyboard, customizing my entire desktop environment, and having complete control over my system was something I genuinely loved.

I also experimented with Fedora and several other distributions along the way.

Apparently, distro-hopping became one of my hobbies!

Fast-forward to today, and I've finally settled on **Void Linux**, running the lightweight `runit` init system and the legendary `dwm` window manager.

I enjoy keeping my system minimal, lightweight, and optimized. My idle RAM usage typically sits somewhere around 500–700 MB on a machine with 16 GB of RAM.

Is that necessary? Probably not.

Is it satisfying? Absolutely!

More importantly, the journey taught me a lot about operating systems, hardware, networking, system administration, troubleshooting, and how computers actually work.

And I wouldn't trade those learning experiences for anything.

## A little more than just Linux

Although Linux and system-level tinkering have been a big part of my journey, my interests aren't limited to operating systems.

I enjoy exploring almost every corner of technology.

Web development is another area I've spent time exploring. I've experimented with different frameworks, tools, and technologies, and I enjoy understanding how things come together to create the websites and applications we use every day.

I'm also interested in DevOps, open-source software, software development, and pretty much anything that involves building, experimenting, or understanding technology.

I enjoy exploring tech products, watching reviews, discovering interesting hardware, and occasionally spending way too much time researching something I probably don't need.

I also love playing video games and exploring different ways to make the gaming experience better.

And when it comes to writing code, my editor of choice is **Neovim**.

Yes, I'm one of those Vim people.

I've grown quite fond of the keyboard-driven workflow, and I genuinely enjoy working in a terminal rather than relying entirely on graphical applications.

I guess the Linux lifestyle has permanently changed me!

## A Jack of All Trades?

If there's one thing that describes my relationship with technology, it's curiosity.

I don't necessarily want to restrict myself to a single technology or specialization. I enjoy exploring different domains, connecting ideas, and learning things that might seem unrelated at first.

Today it might be Linux, tomorrow it could be web development, and the day after that I might find myself researching some obscure piece of hardware.

I like being a beginner at new things. There's something exciting about not knowing how something works and gradually figuring it out.

I wouldn't call myself an expert in everything I explore. There's still an enormous amount I don't know, and honestly, that's one of the things I love about technology.

There's always something new to discover.

## What's next?

I created this website as a place to share things I learn, projects I work on, interesting discoveries, experiments, and occasionally some thoughts about technology.

I don't want this to be just another portfolio website that exists to list a bunch of technologies and projects.

If you're interested in following along, you can subscribe to my newsletter using the email box on the newsletter page. I'll share updates whenever there's something new on the blog, whether it's about Linux, technology, interesting experiments, or upcoming projects.

I'd rather make it a place where I can document my journey, share my experiences, and hopefully help someone else along the way.

I have a lot to learn, a lot to build, and probably a lot of things to break before figuring them out.

So, consider this the beginning.

Thanks for stopping by, and welcome to my little corner of the internet!

**Until next time, keep exploring.**
